Welcome

A plain-language guide to the Grievance Redress Mechanism (eGRM) — for the people who report problems and the people who resolve them.

eGRM is how a problem gets reported, tracked, and fixed. A member of the public raises a complaint; a government officer records it, routes it to the right person, and resolves it. Everyone can see where it stands.

This guide assumes no technical background. Pick the description below that sounds like you.

Which part do you need?

I want to report a problem

Send a complaint from the public website, with or without giving your name, and check on it later. No account needed.

I record complaints from the public

Field Officer — take a complaint in person, by phone or by SMS and enter it into the system correctly.

I assign and resolve complaints

Reviewer or Supervisor — check new complaints, send them to the right officer, and record the outcome.

I set up and run the system

Project Administrator — create a project, load regions, add staff, and decide how complaints are routed.

I work from a phone

The mobile app — record complaints in the field, with or without signal, and see the figures for your area.

How a complaint moves

Every complaint follows the same path, no matter how it arrives:

It is reported

Someone submits it on the public website, or tells a field officer in person, by phone, by SMS, or through the mobile app. The officer records it for them.

It gets a tracking code

The system gives the complaint a code like RDAP-260907-6503. Anyone holding that code can check the status without logging in.

It is reviewed and assigned

A reviewer confirms the details and sends it to the officer or department responsible for that area.

It is resolved

The assigned officer investigates, records what was done, and closes it. The status visible to the public updates at the same time.

About the language

The system opens in Kinyarwanda. The public website also offers English and Français, and you can switch at any time using the selector in the top-right corner.

The public website screenshots in this guide show the English version, so they match the English text around them. The staff screens are shown in Kinyarwanda, because that is what staff accounts on this system actually display. Every staff label is also listed in English on the Screen labels page.
